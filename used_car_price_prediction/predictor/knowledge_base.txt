The used car dataset contains 301 rows and 9 columns: Car_Name, Year, Selling_Price, Present_Price, Driven_kms, Fuel_Type, Selling_type, Transmission, and Owner.

Selling_Price is the target variable. It is the value the machine learning model attempts to predict. The other eight columns are input features.

Present_Price represents the present price of the car in the dataset. Selling_Price and Present_Price are expressed in lakh rupees in the source dataset.

Year is the car's manufacturing year. Driven_kms is the number of kilometres driven. Fuel_Type has Petrol, Diesel, and CNG categories. Selling_type indicates Dealer or Individual. Transmission indicates Manual or Automatic. Owner indicates the number of previous owners.

The project trains Linear Regression and Random Forest Regressor models. It compares them using MAE, RMSE, and R-squared. Lower MAE and RMSE are generally better. R-squared measures the proportion of target variation explained by the model; a value closer to 1 is generally better.

OneHotEncoder converts categorical text values into numeric model features. SimpleImputer provides a fallback for missing feature values. The preprocessing steps and regression algorithm are saved together as a scikit-learn pipeline using Joblib.

A prediction is an estimate based on patterns in a small historical dataset. It is not a guaranteed current market valuation. The dataset contains 301 records and manufacturing years up to 2018.
